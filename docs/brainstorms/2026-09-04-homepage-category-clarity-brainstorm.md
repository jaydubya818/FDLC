# Homepage category clarity and maturity-model UX

## Problem

The homepage headline is memorable, but a first-time visitor can still read the hero without immediately learning what FDLC expands to, what object it governs, or how it differs from SDLC. The system-map panel explains the architecture but omits the Factory Development Lifecycle itself.

The supplied ontology-maturity graphic has a useful information model: a visible journey, a purpose for each level, the capabilities that make the level real, and a concrete outcome. Its poster density, small type, color count, and simultaneous detail are not suitable for a responsive product site.

## Chosen direction

- Keep the existing editorial, industrial visual language and the headline “Build the factory that builds your software.”
- Put the canonical definition in the first paragraph and expand Factory Development Lifecycle before relying on the acronym.
- Place the SDLC / FDLC distinction directly in the hero as a two-row comparison.
- Make “Verified delivery, not merely generated code” the proof-oriented close to the positioning block.
- Turn the right-hand system map into “the factory in one view”: first show the seven-stage Factory Development Lifecycle, then the six architecture areas, then the unit of value.
- Cap the hero's vertical centering on large displays so the category definition appears sooner.
- Apply the strongest lesson from the maturity reference to the maturity page: every level should expose its operating model and the next capability to earn, while the existing assessment supplies the interactive diagnostic. Keep the detail readable rather than reproducing a wall-chart.

## Rejected directions

- Replacing the hero with a dense infographic. It would damage comprehension, mobile usability, and the site's existing visual identity.
- Hiding lifecycle and architecture behind tabs. The homepage needs to teach the category without interaction.
- Adding another animated diagram. Motion would not solve the missing definition and would introduce cognitive load.

## Success criteria

- A visitor can answer “What does FDLC stand for?” and “How is it different from SDLC?” without scrolling.
- The hero visibly connects the lifecycle, architecture, and verified outcome.
- On mobile, the definition precedes the supporting model and no text requires zooming.
- Maturity levels communicate progression and the next practical move without imitating poster-level density.
