## Part 1 — Category survey

### 1. Coding agents and software engineering agents (2026 state)

The 2026 market converged on a shared "harness" feature set (MCP, skills, hooks, subagents, cloud/background execution), which several reviewers now call the primary differentiator rather than the model ([Firecrawl, Aug 2026](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/best-ai-coding-agents)). Honest partial overlaps:

- **GitLab Agentic SDLC** and **GitHub Agent HQ/Agentic Workflows** publish layered governance (policy-as-code, audit, firewalls, golden paths). They are platform-bound; they do not publish a portable artifact model or verification-independence rule. They *could* extend to it, and they have distribution.
- **Augment Code's "software factory"** guide (Aug 2026) is the closest single-vendor articulation: five stages, two mandatory human gates, session-record audit trail, ISO 42001. It lacks factory versioning, multi-factory orchestration, evidence-as-object, and a maturity model.
- **Futurum's Agent Control Plane Framework** states FDLC's control-boundary thesis almost verbatim ("agents decide, control planes govern, execution environments enforce, and systems generate evidence") and demands "runtime, control-plane, and state portability". It is an analyst model without spec, artifacts, or implementation.
- **OWASP ACS** owns "control" at the runtime-hook level and is likely to become the security reference for "Guardian Agents"; FDLC should treat ACS as a lower-layer dependency, not a competitor.
- **Gartner ADLC / Harness Agent DLC / Sierra ADLC** own the phrase "agent development lifecycle" — the lifecycle of *building agents*. FDLC governs the lifecycle of *building software with agents*. These are complementary but will be confused; FDLC must draw the line explicitly.
- **AAIF** has a "Workflows & Process Integration" and a "Taxonomy & Landscape" working group. If those groups publish a lifecycle taxonomy, FDLC's white space narrows; conversely, they are the natural venue to donate or align FDLC vocabulary.
- **DORA** provides the empirical layer (AI amplifies the system; verification tax) that FDLC can cite but does not compete with.

## Conclusion

As of September 2026 the industry has converged on FDLC's premise — the harness, control plane, and evidence around the agent determine outcomes (Anthropic, OpenAI, Thoughtworks, DORA, Futurum, Uber all say so) — but every published lifecycle is vendor-authored and unversioned (Factory.ai, Augment, Port, Cortex, GitLab, AWS AI-DLC, Harness/Gartner ADLC). Standards bodies cover protocols (AAIF: MCP/A2A), runtime control (OWASP ACS), risk (OWASP ASI, MAESTRO, NIST CAISI), and provenance (SLSA), but none covers the software-delivery lifecycle of agent-built software with a typed artifact model, verification independence, and immutable factory versioning. That is FDLC's white space. The threats are vocabulary capture ("software factory", "control plane", "harness", "mission control", "ADLC") and platform gravity (GitHub, GitLab, hyperscaler managed agents). The defense is not more content; it is standards infrastructure — license, RFC process, committee, schemas, conformance, adapters, sponsors, annual data — executed in the next two quarters while the category's vocabulary is still fluid.
