# Essays

## What is it

The essay program is where FDLC argues. The [framework](/framework) and [specification](/specification) define; the [patterns](/patterns) prescribe; essays persuade, with evidence, and are allowed to be wrong in public. Each essay takes one claim from the canon, states it as a thesis, and defends it against the strongest version of the opposing view, citing external data wherever it exists.

Rules for the program:

1. **One thesis per essay**, stated in the first paragraph, defended in the body, restated with its limits at the end.
2. **Argue against the strongest opponent.** Every essay names the best case for the view it rejects.
3. **Evidence with URLs.** DORA, METR, Thoughtworks, Uber, OWASP, Futurum, and vendor primary sources are cited inline. Claims that rest on one secondary source are marked.
4. **One diagram.** Mermaid, labeled with its canon diagram id where one applies.
5. **Canon names only.** Essays use the vocabulary in the [glossary](/glossary); they do not coin terms.
6. **No marketing.** No adjectives about FDLC; no claims about Mission Control without a ledger status.
7. **1,400–2,000 words.** Longer arguments become two essays.

Voice: argumentative, concrete, first person plural where a position is FDLC's, first person singular never. Short sentences. Examples before abstractions.

## Structured data

Each essay is published with `TechArticle` JSON-LD (headline, author as the FDLC.ai Organization, datePublished, dateModified, `about` linking the canon pages, `citation` for each external anchor), `BreadcrumbList` (Home → Essays → title), and a section-specific OG image (see the [SEO plan](/project/seo)).

## Governance

Essays are dated, not versioned. A revised essay keeps its URL and shows a revision note. An essay whose thesis the canon later rejects is not deleted; it gains a header noting the change and linking the decision record.
